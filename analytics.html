I turned those problems into blogs written for small-business owners, in their own words, and each one shows how SocialAddress solves the problem.

03 / 0402 · Google Analytics

How did I use analytics to understand users?

Where users came from, where they went, and where they quit.

Scroll to flip ↻
03 / 04

Interviews told me what users said. Google Analytics showed me what they actually did.

How I used it

Check trackingMade sure key actions were recorded correctly
Follow trafficWhere users came from and went next
Find drop-offsIn sign-up and store creation
Spot unused featuresFeatures users never found
Ask whyUser calls about what felt confusing

What it showedUsers quit in two places: inside key flows, like sign-up and store creation, and before they ever found a feature. Analytics showed where they quit; user calls showed why.

For Shop, I also defined the analytics requirements and set up event tracking, so the team could see what merchants did, not only what they said.

04 / 0403 · Onboarding improvements

How did I reduce onboarding friction?

Guidance at the exact moments users got stuck.

Scroll to flip ↻
04 / 04

The analytics and user calls pointed to two kinds of friction. Users got stuck inside a flow, like sign-up or creating a store, or they never discovered a feature at all. Using those findings, we added guidance at the moments users needed it.

Product tours

Walk new users through the main features the first time they arrive.

Tooltips

Short hints at the steps where users got stuck, like store creation.

Easier to find

Underused features made more visible, so users could discover more of the platform.

Next stepMeasure each change against the drop-off it targeted. For example, comparing store-creation completion before and after the tooltips would show which changes actually helped.

I don’t have verified numbers on the impact of these changes, so this page shows none.
