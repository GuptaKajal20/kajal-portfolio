Shop: from PDF catalogues to a store merchants run themselves

Small sellers ran every order by hand, and a full online store felt too heavy for them.

RoleProduct Management Intern
TimelineJan – Aug 2026
My partResearch, PRD, scope, flows, testing, pilot onboarding
TeamFounders, Designer, Engineering, Sales & Ops
01Problem

Every order was run by hand.

  • A typical order: PDF catalogue on chat → customer picks a code → back-and-forth on price and delivery → UPI screenshot checked by hand.
  • Shopify-style stores felt too technical and too costly, so most had no website at all.
02Discovery

The barrier was effort, not desire.

  • 44 merchant interviews, asking how they sold their last product, not what features they wanted.
  • Of 39 product sellers: 15 had no website, 10 found setup too technical or slow, 8 named cost.
  • My fixed-template idea was rejected: merchants wanted the store to look like their brand, because that builds trust.
03Approach

Counted themes, then grouped them into three problems.

  • Tagged each interview by theme and counted how often each came up.
  • Grouped them into setup effort, trust and manual payments.
  • Counter-signal kept in view: 12 of 39 (made-to-order, wholesale) don't fit a fixed store, so V1 targets ready-stock sellers.
04Solution

A branded store that completes one real order end to end.

  • Pick a template, then add your own logo, colours and fonts.
  • Payment to the merchant's UPI ID instead of a gateway: no setup, at the cost of payouts within 24 hours.
  • Coupons, campaigns and abandoned-cart reminders moved to V2, since they only matter after a first sale.
Live

Launched with 10 pilot merchants. Feedback: they could set up and run the store themselves. I have no verified usage numbers, so I don't show any.
