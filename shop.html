Sellers wanted to sell online, but a website felt like expert work.

Merchants sold on Instagram and WhatsApp: product posts and stories, PDF catalogues in chat, and order details sent message by message. When they wanted a proper online store, Shopify and Wix felt too complicated and slow to set up.

Problem statementMerchants who want to sell online can’t get a store up without time, cost and technical effort that don’t fit where their business is.

03 / 0602 · Discovery & Insights

How did I validate the problem?

40+ merchant interviews about how they sell today.

Scroll to flip ↻
03 / 06

The first idea was a simple drag-and-drop storefront. Before building it, I interviewed 40+ merchants, mostly product sellers on Instagram. Instead of asking what features they wanted, I asked them to walk me through how they sold their last product, from first message to delivery.

How one order happens today

StepWhat the merchant doesPain point
1Posts products on Instagram, with prices in captionsCustomers still DM for links and prices
2Replies to every DM by handHours a day, mixed with collab spam
3Shares a PDF catalogue; the customer picks a code or sends a screenshotNo real browsing; every order is quoted by hand
4Takes payment by UPI and checks the screenshotManual checks; trust issues on both sides
5Arranges delivery and sends updates on WhatsAppTracked in Excel or a notebook
6Tries to set up Shopify or Wix to growThemes, variants, payments and domains feel like expert work, so they stop or pay a developer

Three insights

1 · Setup is the barrier, not desire

Of 39 product sellers, 15 had no website, 10 found setup too technical or slow and 8 named cost. A developer was estimated at ₹10,000–15,000.

2 · Trust comes from the brand

Merchants wanted a store that looks like their own brand, because customers trust that more than a chat.

3 · Payments are manual

UPI screenshots, part-advance or cash on delivery, checked one by one.

Who we built for. Riya Mehta (fictional persona built from several real interviews) sells handmade candles and hampers on Instagram, takes orders in DMs, tried Shopify and gave up on themes, variants and payments.

Counter-signal. Made-to-order, wholesale and custom sellers need a conversation, not a fixed store, so we focused on ready-stock sellers.

Resulting goalMerchants didn’t lack a store builder; they lacked an easy one. So we set one goal: a merchant should be able to set up a store and start selling in about 5 minutes, with no developer.

MVP scope: built, deferred, out of scope

DecisionStatusWhy
Structured storefront with optional sectionsBuiltMerchants didn’t know what a website needs. Banner, category tabs, products, collections, Social Highlights and FAQs give a starting point; they keep only what they want.
Social HighlightsBuiltA merchant shared customer posts in Instagram Stories as social proof. This puts them on her own store.
Limited customisationBuiltToo many choices overwhelm new merchants and slow setup. Merchants choose only a theme (classic, gradient or glass), one accent colour that becomes a full palette, a card style and fonts.
Ready-made templates by business typeBuiltPick one, swap in your own images and content.
Connect a payment account, or use a UPI IDBuiltA home seller like Riya rarely has a payment-gateway account, and she already takes payment by UPI. A UPI ID let her start without gateway setup. Trade-off: UPI payouts within 24 hours, handled by the team.
Use a delivery partner, or deliver yourselfBuiltRiya makes candles at home and often sells locally, so she may not have a delivery partner. Self-delivery lets her sell in her own area without one; merchants who ship further can choose a partner.
Platform-written order emailsBuiltNothing extra to set up; customers still get status updates.
Coupons, campaigns, abandoned-cart remindersDeferredThey grow sales, which only matters after a first sale.
Custom email templatesDeferredOnly if merchants ask for it.
Made-to-order and wholesale flowsOut of scopeThey need a conversation, not a fixed store.

Every decision was checked against one question: can a merchant complete one real order without leaving the platform?

05 / 0604 · Building & Launching Shop

How did I take the product forward?

From PRD and flows to a live storefront.

Scroll to flip ↻
05 / 06

I wrote the PRD and mapped the merchant and customer flows, from sign-up to a delivered order. I decided the storefront structure and built the base template and its business-type versions; the designer turned them into high-fidelity UI. With engineering, I turned the payment provider’s documentation into requirements and worked on CSV product import. I also defined analytics requirements and set up event tracking, so the team could see what merchants did, not only what they said. The pilot merchants were existing SocialAddress clients, and we spoke with them to gather feedback.

What the customer experiences

BrowseThe merchant’s store
Add to cartSizes and colours

One example from the work

Designing for Riya, not for big brands. Most store builders assume a merchant already has a payment gateway and a courier partner. Riya has neither: she takes UPI and often delivers locally herself. So I made both optional: connect a payment account or just add a UPI ID, and choose a delivery partner or deliver yourself. That removed two setup steps that would have stopped home sellers from going live, at the cost of the team handling UPI payouts by hand during the pilot.

06 / 0605 · Outcomes & Learnings

What happened, and what did I learn?

Live with 10 pilot merchants, and what I'd measure next.

Scroll to flip ↻
06 / 06
Outcome

Shop launched with 10 pilot merchants, existing SocialAddress clients. Their main feedback: they could set up and run the store themselves. We then connected the SAM AI agent to draft product descriptions, and explored SEO and GEO so products could appear in search (an early, partial implementation).

Learning

What someone asks for isn’t always the real problem. We were asked for a shop; merchants needed selling to stop being so much work.

Next step

Measure stores published, time to first product and first order, and time the 5-minute goal with real merchants.

My internship ended before longer-term results were tracked, so this page shows no usage, order or revenue numbers.
